Chapter 21 · Deep Learning

Chapter 19 learned from five features a human designed. A neural network gets only the raw 5×5 neighbourhood around a square: for each cell, is it inside the cave, has it been observed, was there a breeze, was there a stench (100 inputs). It must discover useful features in its hidden layer on its own. Everything here, including backpropagation and the Adam optimizer, is written from scratch and trains live in your browser.

Chapter notes & questions
  1. With 0 hidden units the network is logistic regression on raw inputs. What does a hidden layer add, and how much, on this task?
  2. Look at the receptive fields. Can you find a unit that responds to “breeze right next to the centre”? One that responds to breezes two squares away?
  3. Why can't the network beat the exact posterior's loss, however big it is? What is the exact posterior, in learning terms?
  4. Raise the learning rate to 0.2. What happens to the loss curve, and why?
  5. The same weights are applied at every square. That is weight sharing, the idea behind convolutional networks (§21.3).
