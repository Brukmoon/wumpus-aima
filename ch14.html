Chapter 14 · Probabilistic Reasoning over Time

Now the wumpus wanders: at each step it stays put or moves to a random pit-free neighbour. A flying sensor drone patrols the cave and reports a noisy stench reading every step. Where is the wumpus now (filtering)? Where was it (smoothing)? What path did it most likely take (Viterbi)? Can a few hundred samples keep track of it (particle filtering)?

Chapter notes & questions
  1. Why is smoothing never worse than filtering on average? At which time steps does it help most?
  2. Set P(stench | not near) = 0.5, so a stench carries almost no information. What happens to the filter? What about a perfect sensor (0.99 / 0.01)?
  3. The Viterbi path is the most likely sequence. Can it pass through a square that the smoothed distribution thinks unlikely at that time? (§14.2.3)
  4. Try 20 particles and then 2000. Where does the particle filter lose the wumpus, and why doesn't it recover by itself?
  5. This is the same machinery as robot localization (§14.3), with the roles swapped: here the sensor's position is known and the target is hidden.
