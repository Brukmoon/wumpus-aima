Chapter 17 · Making Complex Decisions

A sequential decision problem: the cave is known, but the floor is slippery. Each move goes where intended with probability 1 − slip, otherwise sideways, like the book's 4×3 world. The gold ends the episode with +1, a pit or the wumpus with −1, and every step earns a living reward. The solution is a policy: what to do in every square, because the agent can't be sure where it will end up.

Try the book's regimes (Fig. 17.2): very negative (the agent hurries, even into a pit), around −0.04 (it detours for safety), and positive (it avoids the exit forever).

And the real Wumpus World? A POMDP (§17.4)

Here the agent sees the whole cave. In the real game it doesn't know where the pits are, so the problem is a partially observable MDP. Its “state” is then a belief state, a probability distribution over caves, which is exactly the posterior computed in Chapter 12. The Chapter 12 agent is a simple, myopic approximation of a POMDP policy. It acts on the current belief without planning how future percepts will change it. A full POMDP solution would also value actions for the information they bring (the VPI of Chapter 16).
