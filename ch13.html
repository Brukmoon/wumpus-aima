Chapter 13 · Probabilistic Reasoning

The Wumpus World as a Bayesian network: a Pit node for each square, pointing into the Breeze node of each neighbour. The book's breeze sensor is deterministic. Here you can also make it noisy (missed or false breezes) with a noisy-OR CPT, and see how exact inference and three sampling algorithms cope.

CPT: P(Breeze = true | pits) = 1 − (1 − leak) · (1 − q)number of neighbouring pits. With q = 1 and leak = 0 this is the book's deterministic sensor. Click a frontier square on the grid to query it.

Chapter notes & questions
  1. With the deterministic sensor, how many samples does rejection sampling accept? Why does it get worse as you observe more squares?
    hintThe probability of the evidence shrinks exponentially with the number of evidence variables (§13.4.2).
  2. Likelihood weighting never rejects. Look at the effective sample size: why can it still be poor?
  3. Which pit nodes are irrelevant to the query, and why can they be dropped without changing the answer (§13.4)?
  4. Identify the Markov blanket of the query node. Gibbs sampling only needs that blanket to resample the node. Why?
  5. Make the sensor noisy (q = 0.9, leak = 0.1). How do the exact posteriors change? Do squares that were “certainly safe” stay certain?
