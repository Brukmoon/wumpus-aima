Chapter notes & questions
  1. With ε = 0 (pure greedy) the agent often settles on a poor route forever. Why? This is the exploration–exploitation dilemma (§22.3.1).
  2. Switch to the exploration function f(u, n): try each action N_e times, then act greedily. Why does it often get stuck looping near the start here, when ε-greedy does not?
    hintAfter N_e tries it never explores again. In a slippery world the first few tries of a good action can be unlucky (a slide into a pit), and with a decaying α those early estimates take a long time to fix.
  3. Q-learning vs SARSA with ε-greedy exploration: which one learns the optimal policy, and which one learns a policy that is safer while exploring? Look at squares next to pits.
  4. Squares the agent rarely visits keep bad Q-values (look at the visit counts). Does that matter for the policy from [1,1]?
  5. A constant α = 0.5 learns fast but never settles. Why does a decaying α(n) converge?
  6. This agent needs thousands of episodes, each ending in the gold, a pit or the wumpus. Compare that with the model-based agents of earlier chapters, which knew the rules of the world.
