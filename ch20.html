Chapter 20 · Learning Probabilistic Models

Every earlier chapter assumed the pit probability p = 0.2 was known. Where would an agent get such numbers? From data. This chapter learns the parameters of the Wumpus model: from fully observed caves (Bayesian learning), as a classifier (naive Bayes), and from caves where the pits are never seen, only their breezes (EM).

Chapter notes & questions
  1. Bayesian learning: with a Beta(1,1) prior, how many worlds until the posterior is narrower than ±0.05? What does a strong wrong prior, like Beta(20,2), do early on and later?
  2. The maximum-likelihood estimate after one world can be 0. Why is that dangerous for an agent, and how does the Bayesian posterior mean avoid it?
  3. Naive Bayes assumes the features are independent given the class. They are not (breezy and calm neighbours are related). Look at the calibration chart: where does that assumption hurt?
  4. EM never sees a pit, yet it recovers p. Which information in the breeze maps makes that possible? What would happen if every world had zero breezes?
  5. EM's log likelihood never decreases between iterations (§20.3). Check it in the chart. Does EM reach the same answer from p₀ = 0.05 and from p₀ = 0.7?
